CCaaS migration to Salesforce without the go-live weekend nobody sleeps through

Written by Sean Shvarzman · Updated October 2026

Moving a contact center from Genesys, Five9, NICE CXone, Avaya or a standalone Amazon Connect onto Salesforce is mostly a routing, IVR and change problem, not a software problem. I plan it as waves by queue, keep reporting whole, and cut over when the numbers say so.

What a CCaaS migration to Salesforce involves

A CCaaS migration to Salesforce replaces or integrates your contact center platform so that calls, messaging and cases are routed, handled and reported in Service Cloud. The telephony lands on Service Cloud Voice, either bundled Amazon Connect or your existing platform through partner telephony. The agent desktop becomes the Service Console. IVRs, queues, skills, recordings and reports are redesigned rather than copied.

The two big architectural choices are whether to keep the existing CCaaS as the telephony layer under Salesforce or to retire it, and whether to rebuild the IVR as a shorter, intent-first flow or preserve it during the move. Everything else, from number porting to agent training, follows from those two.

When a migration is the wrong first move

If the service team is not on Salesforce yet, or the current CCaaS contract has years left and the platform works, a full migration is not where to start. Fixing routing, IVR length and the agent desktop in place usually pays back sooner, and it makes the eventual move smaller. I will say which situation you are in during the first call.

Where migrations go wrong

  • One big-bang cutover. Waves by queue let you fix routing on a small group before the whole floor is on the new desktop.
  • Reporting discovered after go-live. Supervisors need the same numbers on day one. Parity is a workstream, not a cleanup.
  • Call history nobody can find. Decide early what is migrated, what is archived and what is retained for compliance.
  • Training on screenshots. Agents need the real console with real queues in a sandbox weeks before their wave.
Questions

Frequently asked questions

Should we keep our CCaaS as the telephony layer or replace it?

If your contract has time left and the platform's telephony is solid, keeping it as partner telephony under Service Cloud Voice gets agents into one console sooner. If the contract is ending or you want one vendor, Service Cloud Voice with Amazon Connect removes a platform. I model both with your contract dates and volumes before recommending.

How long does a CCaaS migration to Salesforce take?

A single-site migration with a modest IVR typically runs three to six months from assessment to final wave. Multi-site, multi-carrier programs with compliance recording run longer, and the wave plan is built around porting lead times and seasonal peaks.

Do we lose call recordings and reports from the old system?

Not if it is planned. Recordings are archived or retained per your compliance rules, historical reports are preserved as exports or in a warehouse, and new reporting is built to match the metrics supervisors already use before the first wave.

Can we migrate in phases?

Yes, and you should. I migrate by queue or business unit, run the old and new platforms in parallel for each wave, and move the next group only when the agreed KPIs hold.

What happens to our IVR?

It is redesigned, not copied. Most legacy IVRs are longer than they need to be. The new flow identifies the customer and the reason quickly, resolves what self-service can, and routes the rest to the right skill with context.
