The formula, in full

Agent hours freed per month = calls per month × 30% deflection × average handle time (minutes) ÷ 60.
Annual cost recovered = hours freed × $35 per hour × 12, rounded to the nearest $1,000.

With the default inputs: 20,000 calls × 0.30 × 8 minutes ÷ 60 = 800 hours per month. 800 × $35 × 12 = $336,000 per year. The agent count does not change the estimate; it is there so you can sanity-check the hours against your team size. 800 hours is roughly five full-time agents' monthly capacity.

What the assumptions mean

  • 30% deflection of routine contacts. A conservative starting point from programs I have delivered on Salesforce Service Cloud, Service Cloud Voice and Agentforce. Your number depends on how many contacts are routine, how good the knowledge base is, and which channels the automation runs on.
  • $35 fully loaded hourly cost. Wages plus benefits, management and tooling for a US agent, as a round number. Offshore or specialist teams will differ.
  • Average handle time. Use the handle time of the contacts you would automate, not the blended average, if you know it. Routine contacts are often shorter than average.

How to turn this into a real business case

  1. Pull contact reasons from your real data and tag which ones are routine.
  2. Set a deflection target per reason, not one number for everything.
  3. Decide what happens to freed hours: redeploy, absorb growth, or reduce.
  4. Add licensing and implementation costs, then the revenue and satisfaction effects that this calculator leaves out.

That is most of what the first two weeks of a discovery engagement produce. If you want to shortcut it, book a 30-minute call and bring your call volumes.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

How is the estimate calculated?

Agent hours freed per month equals calls per month, times a 30% deflection rate, times average handle time in minutes, divided by 60. Annual cost recovered equals those hours times a $35 fully loaded hourly cost, times 12 months, rounded to the nearest thousand.

Why 30% deflection?

It is a conservative starting assumption for routine contacts in programs I have delivered. Real rates vary by intent mix, knowledge quality and channel. During discovery we replace it with a target per contact reason.

Why $35 per hour?

It is a rough fully loaded cost for a US contact center agent including wages, benefits, management and tooling. Replace it with your own number for a real estimate; the formula above makes that a one-line change.

What does the calculator leave out?

Licensing and implementation costs, the revenue side of faster service, customer satisfaction effects, and the agent capacity you may redeploy rather than remove. A business case covers all of those; this tool only sizes the labor side of deflection.

Where do the agents go when hours are freed?

In most programs the hours are redeployed to complex work, backlog, sales or outbound rather than removed. Which of those applies to you is a discovery question, and it changes the business case more than the deflection rate does.
