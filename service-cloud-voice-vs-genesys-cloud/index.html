Service Cloud Voice vs Genesys Cloud: how to decide

Written by Sean Shvarzman · Updated October 2026

The question is rarely which platform is better. It is where your agents should work, where routing should live, and whether you are consolidating or keeping a telephony platform you already run well. Here is how I frame it with clients.

What each one is

Service Cloud Voice is the integration of a telephony system with Salesforce: calls routed by Omni-Channel next to chats and cases, answered in the Service Console, transcribed and stored on the customer record. Genesys Cloud is a standalone contact center platform with its own routing engine, agent desktop, IVR, workforce engagement and analytics, which can integrate with Salesforce, including as partner telephony underneath Service Cloud Voice.

That last clause matters. For a Salesforce service team the realistic options are three, not two: Service Cloud Voice with Amazon Connect, Genesys Cloud as the telephony layer under Service Cloud Voice, or Genesys Cloud as the primary platform with Salesforce as the CRM behind it. Salesforce's newer Agentforce Contact Center adds a fourth, fully native path, covered on the Service Cloud Voice page.

The consolidation question

Most Salesforce service teams I meet are not choosing between the two from scratch. They run Genesys today and are deciding whether to keep it. The honest answer depends on three things: how much of the Genesys estate is actually used beyond routing and recording, when the contract renews, and whether supervisors already live in Salesforce reports or in Genesys analytics.

  • Keep Genesys as partner telephony when workforce engagement or complex routing is genuinely in use and the contract has time left. Agents still get one console in Salesforce.
  • Move to Service Cloud Voice with Amazon Connect when Genesys is mostly a phone switch for a team that already works in Salesforce, and the renewal is approaching.
  • Stay Genesys-primary when the contact center serves several CRMs or heavy outsourcers, and Salesforce is one system among many.

What migrating looks like

From Genesys to Service Cloud Voice, the work is the IVR redesign, routing in Omni-Channel, number porting, recording retention and reporting parity, migrated in waves by queue. Keeping Genesys underneath is faster to stand up but leaves two routing models unless you deliberately simplify. I cover the full sequence on the CCaaS migration page.

What to verify in a scripted demo

Vendor capabilities change every quarter, so treat this page as a way to decide, not a feature matrix. Before choosing, run the same script on both platforms with your own call types:

  1. An identified customer calls about an open case. Where does the agent land, and how many clicks to the case?
  2. A call and a chat arrive for the same skill. Which system decides who gets what, and can a supervisor see both queues in one place?
  3. Transfer a call with context. What does the receiving agent see?
  4. Pull the after-call summary, the transcript and the recording. Where does each live, and who can report on them?
  5. Turn on an AI agent for one routine intent. How is it grounded, and how does it hand off?
Questions

Frequently asked questions

Can Genesys Cloud run underneath Service Cloud Voice?

Yes. Genesys offers a partner telephony integration for Service Cloud Voice, so agents work in the Salesforce console while Genesys handles the calls. It is a common first step before deciding whether to consolidate.

Is Service Cloud Voice a full replacement for Genesys Cloud?

For a team that works in Salesforce and needs voice, digital routing, transcription and AI, yes. Teams that rely on Genesys's workforce engagement suite or serve several CRMs should evaluate what they would replace it with before deciding.

Which is cheaper?

It depends on licences you already hold, contract timing and how many platforms you retire. I model total cost with contract dates rather than list prices, and the answer changes from client to client.

How long does a switch from Genesys to Service Cloud Voice take?

A single-site migration with a modest IVR typically runs three to six months from assessment to final wave. Number porting and reporting parity usually set the pace.
